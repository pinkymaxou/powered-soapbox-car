Simulation assumptions

Model

  • Controller: the real firmware logic (controller_core.cpp, identical to the kart) at 500 Hz — same config defaults (PARAMS), same hw:: thresholds.
  • Chassis: planar rigid body with 2 degrees of freedom (longitudinal speed + yaw). The wheels do not slip laterally (side-slip only appears as yaw damping).
  • Rollover: quasi-static criterion — tipping about the front-wheel↔caster-wheel edge of the support triangle. Effective half-width at the CG: w_eff = (track/2)·(1 − x_cg/wheelbase); limit a_tip = g·w_eff/h_cg. Suspension/tire transients are ignored.
  • Motors: linear DC (kt = ke), current i = (V − ke·ω)/Ra, clamped to ±19.6 A per motor — the theoretical capacity of the MOTOR (12 V / ~19.6 A, ~172 W, below the driver limit of 20 A continuous / 60 A peak): torque, force, battery draw and energy can never exceed it, including at stall and in plugging (V + back-EMF across Ra would give ~150 A without this bound). Max force at the wheel ≈ 52 N per motor. Dynamic braking = short circuit (V = 0, current through Ra).
  • Battery: ideal source + internal resistance (V = V₀ − R·I). No discharge model.
  • Sensors: AS5600 quantized to 12 bits ×1.28 (like the real one); perfect gamepad (the script speaks in calibrated setpoint); injectable failures (missing/inverted/frozen/aberrant).
  • Power cut (kill switch): the controller stops running and the bridges open → FLOATING motors, no electrical force — only rolling against the slope remains (scenarios coupure_pente8/16: runaway demonstrated).

Motor capacity and slope holding

  • Max drive force: i clamped to 19.6 A → wheel torque = kt·i·16·0.85 → ≈ 52 N per wheel, ≈ 104 N total (+ ~30 N of rolling that helps hold).
  • Maximum holdable slope (loaded kart 98 kg): m·g·sin θ ≤ 134 N → ≈ 11%. Below that (8%: 77 N), the PID brake holds near a stop; beyond that (16%: 152 N), the kart SLIDES even with the brake active — the descente16_* scenarios demonstrate it. A steep slope requires a mechanical brake.
  • Clamped dynamic braking: in short circuit, i = ke·ω/Ra reaches 19.6 A around ~0.9 m/s — beyond that, its force is CONSTANT (~104 N), it no longer grows with speed.

Energy (estimate) — how the calculation is done

  • Current of each motor: i = (Vapplied − ke·ωmotor) / Ra, with Vapplied = signed PWM × duty cap × Vterminals and ωmotor = (vwheel/rwheel) × reduction.
  • Terminal voltage: Vterminals = V₀ − Rint·(|iL| + |iR|) — the battery sag under load is therefore included in the calculation.
  • Battery power: P = Vterminals × (|iL| + |iR|) during traction and PID braking (plugging draws real battery current); P = 0 during dynamic braking — the short circuit drives the current through the motor's Ra, the energy dissipates as motor heat, not from the battery.
  • Cumulative energy: E = ∫ P·dt, integrated at 500 Hz, displayed in Wh.
  • Limits of the estimate: no recovery (regeneration) modeled, driver and wiring losses ignored, |i| summed without sign (an honest upper bound for an H-bridge without grid feedback). It is an order of magnitude to compare scenarios, not a battery charge balance.

Masses and center of gravity

  • Total mass ≈ 98 kg: kart ≈ 32 kg (wooden chassis, motors, battery at the nose, electronics) + passengers ≈ 66 kg — two children about 10 years old (~33 kg each), seated side by side on the bench.
  • Center of gravity assumed FIXED: x_cg ≈ 0.40 m behind the drive axle (the children sit toward the rear, the battery in the nose pulls the CG forward), h_cg ≈ 0.38 m above the ground (low seating ~20 cm, children's torsos above).
  • Geometric consequence (tricycle): the effective support half-width at the CG is w_eff = (track/2)·(1 − x_cg/wheelbase) ≈ 0.42 × (1 − 0.40/0.765) ≈ 0.20 m → tip limit a_tip = g·w_eff/h_cg ≈ 5.2 m/s².
  • Offset load modeled (y_cg): tipping becomes asymmetric — the edge on the loaded side loses its margin. The enfant_seul_cote and adulte_enfant scenarios show that with the DEFAULT settings an offset CG can tip over in an extreme maneuver; the iso-a_lat limit (∝ 1/v, turn_at_vmax = 0.2, turn gain 1.0) also protects these cases.
  • Model limitation: passengers leaning shift the real CG dynamically — which is why the tests require a margin and not just "does not tip over".
