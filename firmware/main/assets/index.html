🎓 Control modes

The kart turns the stick into a left/right motor command (arcade mixing: forward drives both wheels, turn adds a left/right difference). What sits between the stick and the motors is what changes between the two modes.

🔒 Closed loop — normal driving

Every safety and comfort layer is active; the encoders measure wheel speed.

Stick→ Deadzone→ Turn slew→ Rollover limit→ Arcade mix→ Speed PID (enc)→ PWM cap→ Motors
🔧 Open loop — bench / stand testing

Raw passthrough: only the PWM cap and the safety gates remain — no speed limit, no rollover protection, no smoothing, no PID braking.

⚙️ Gear & sprocket ratios — motor to wheel, per side

Each front wheel has its own drivetrain: a printed two-stage gearbox, then a #35 roller chain to the wheel. What makes two reductions fit in one small box is the compound gear — the 80T of stage 1 and the 30T of stage 2 are a single printed part on one shaft. Plan view; gears and sprockets are drawn proportional to their pitch diameters at the real centre distances, only the chain run is shortened to fit:

Two things the drawing is telling you. The two 80T overlap because they really do overlap seen from above — they live in different tooth planes, one above the other, which is what sets the depth of the housing and why nothing may stick out into the neighbouring plane. And the 25T sprocket is nearly as big as the 80T gear next to it: #35 chain has a 3/8″ (9.53 mm) pitch against the gearbox's 1.06 mm module, so a sprocket tooth is roughly nine times the pitch of a gear tooth.

StageTeethRatioCumulative
1 — pinion → gear16T → 80T (24 DP, metal pinion)5.000 : 15.00
2 — compound → output30T → 80T (30T is one part with the stage-1 80T)2.667 : 113.33

Speed: 4615 rpm ÷ 17.07 ≈ 270 rpm at the wheel, × π × 0.254 m ≈ 3.6 m/s unloaded at 12 V (~3.4 m/s in practice) — well above what the firmware allows, the real cap is speed_limit_ms. Torque multiplies the same way in reverse: ~0.36 N·m at the motor becomes ~6 N·m at the wheel, which is why stage 2 is the thick one.

Why a chain and not a belt. A chain is cut to whatever length you need — add or drop links, close it with a master link — so the gearbox-to-wheel centre distance stays a free variable instead of being dictated by the belt lengths that happen to exist. On a one-off build where the motor mount finds its final position during assembly, that settles it. The counterpart: unlike a belt a chain does not damp shocks (they go straight into the gear teeth) and it wants lubrication, alignment and re-tensioning — a slack chain climbs the sprocket flank and is thrown.

🔋 Battery measurement — the divider bridge into the ADS1115

The pack sits far above anything an ADC can swallow, so a resistor divider scales it down onto A0 of the external ADS1115. That converter is used instead of the ESP32's own ADC because it is 16-bit, linear, and — decisively — because ADC2 is unusable while Wi-Fi is running.

The green figures are live, from what the converter is actually reading (15 s sliding median, same value as the Dashboard diagram) — V_adc at the node and the pack voltage rebuilt from it. They read — when the ADS1115 is not detected.

The divider gives V_adc = Vbat × 15 / (100 + 15) = Vbat / 7.667, and the firmware goes the other way: Vbat = V_adc × 7.667. That ratio is not a settable parameter — it is hw::VBAT_DIV_RATIO, computed from the two resistor values in control_types.hpp, because it is decided by what is soldered on the board and not by taste. Change the resistors, change those two constants, reflash.

Change the pack, change the resistors. 100 k/15 k suits a 12 V pack (1.93 V at A0 when charging at 14.8 V). A 24 V pack would drive A0 to 3.8 V — past the converter's absolute maximum — so it needs 100 k/12 k (ratio 9.33); a 12 V pack can also use 100 k/27 k (ratio 4.70) to use more of the ADC range.

Why it is only read at 20 Hz. The ADS1115 shares I²C bus 0 with the left wheel's AS5600. Polling it on every 500 Hz control tick stretched the interval between two encoder reads just enough to alias the left wheel's speed — measured stdev fell from 1313 to 117 once the battery moved to its own slow cadence. Vbat changes over seconds; the encoder does not.

What the firmware does with it. The pack type is detected at boot — the voltage must hold steady within 0.5 V for 3 s, then it is classed 12 V or 24 V on an 18 V threshold and frozen until reboot. Thresholds are hardcoded per type rather than left as parameters, so they cannot be set inconsistently: 12 V → warn 11.5 / cut 10.5 / rearm 12.0 V; 24 V → 23.0 / 21.0 / 24.0 V. The cut is debounced (~0.5 s) so a momentary sag under load does not stop the kart, and coming back up requires a deliberate rearm on START.
