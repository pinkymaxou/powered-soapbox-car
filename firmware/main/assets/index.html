💡 LED strip — what the colors mean

Moving rainbow = armed & ready (hold A to drive) · yellow = healthy but disarmed (pulses while the START hold is in progress) · blinking red = gamepad connected but not calibrated, so START does nothing (2 s rapid burst, then 1 Hz until it is done — never solid) · blue = gamepad calibration in progress.

🎓 How it drives

Nothing on the kart measures speed. The stick is mixed (arcade: forward drives both wheels, turn adds a left/right difference) and goes straight to the motors: no speed loop, no turn limiter, no smoothing. duty_cap is the only bound.

Stick→ Deadzone→ Mix (linear / expo)→ Accel / decel (%/s)→ PWM cap→ Motors

A held, stick centredPseudo-freewheel: the command simply targets 0 and slides there at decel_pct_s (0 = straight to the brake).
BE-stop: disarm + dynamic brake. Release it, rearm with START.

Two slopes, one idea. The stick sets a target; accel_pct_s caps how fast the PWM may rise toward it and decel_pct_s how fast it may fall back, both in % of full duty per second. 200 %/s = half a second from standstill to full power; 100 %/s = one second to come back down. The rise is there for the motor drivers: a stick slammed open is a step from 0 to full duty, and a brushed motor answers that with its stall current. 0 disables either side.

Why "pseudo"-freewheel. The Cytron MDD20A has no coast state: PWM low grounds both motor outputs, and that is the brake. So a real freewheel cannot be had — it is imitated by the fall slope: with A held and the stick centred the target is simply 0, and the command slides there at decel_pct_s instead of dropping, so the motor neither pushes nor brakes much on the way. That is why there is no separate run-down setting any more: a run-down is a deceleration rate. Once at 0 it is electrically the brake, and the readout says DYNAMIC rather than pretending to still coast. Flat ground only: nothing measures a slope.

Neither slope delays the brake. Releasing A, disarming, B, a lost or silent gamepad: all of those ground the windings on the tick they happen, whatever the ramps are doing. The slopes only shape a command the driver is still asking for.

Simulated, from full speed on the flat: A released → ~1.6 m/s half a second later, then stopped · freewheel → still ~1.9 m/s at that same half second, stopped once the run-down ends · e-stop mushroom (power cut, true freewheel) → coasts ~10.7 m. With A released on a slope the kart creeps at ~0.2 m/s (8 %) or ~0.5 m/s (16 %); a power cut on a slope runs away.

🛑 Limits & safeties

Always on: disarmed, B, gamepad lost or silent for 750 ms, gamepad not calibrated → disarm + dynamic brake. Driving needs a calibrated gamepad; the kart disarms itself after disarm_s without driving. The gamepad buzzes when the stick is pushed while not armed — not when armed with A released, which is the normal way to brake — and gives a soft pulse on arming and a strong one on B.

No rollover protection — it went with the encoders, by the owner's decision; duty_cap is the only lever. Simulation, full-speed full turn (18T gearing): two children centred stay planted at 1.0; one child sitting off-centre, or an adult + a child, tip at 1.0 and stay upright at 0.9; 0.8 gives about the margin the old protection had. The default stays 1.0. On the flat with 40 lb pellet bags aboard the owner could not tip it — the bags sit lower than a seated child, and the model has never been checked against a real tip test.

⚙️ Gear & sprocket ratios — motor to wheel, per side

Each rear (driven) wheel has its own drivetrain: a printed two-stage gearbox, then a #35 roller chain to the wheel. What makes two reductions fit in one small box is the compound gear — the 80T of stage 1 and the 30T of stage 2 are a single printed part on one shaft. Plan view; gears and sprockets are drawn proportional to their pitch diameters at the real centre distances, only the chain run is shortened to fit:

Two things the drawing is telling you. The two 80T overlap because they really do overlap seen from above — they live in different tooth planes, one above the other, which is what sets the depth of the housing and why nothing may stick out into the neighbouring plane. And the 18T sprocket is two-thirds the size of the 80T gear next to it: #35 chain has a 3/8″ (9.53 mm) pitch against the gearbox's 1.06 mm module, so a sprocket tooth is roughly nine times the pitch of a gear tooth.

Speed: 4615 rpm ÷ 23.70 ≈ 195 rpm at the wheel, × π × 0.254 m ≈ 2.6 m/s unloaded at 12 V (~2.5 m/s loaded, in simulation). Nothing in the firmware caps it but duty_cap. Torque multiplies the same way in reverse: ~0.36 N·m at the motor becomes ~8.5 N·m at the wheel, which is why stage 2 is the thick one.

Why a chain and not a belt. A chain is cut to whatever length you need — add or drop links, close it with a master link — so the gearbox-to-wheel centre distance stays a free variable instead of being dictated by the belt lengths that happen to exist. On a one-off build where the motor mount finds its final position during assembly, that settles it. The counterpart: unlike a belt a chain does not damp shocks (they go straight into the gear teeth) and it wants lubrication, alignment and re-tensioning — a slack chain climbs the sprocket flank and is thrown.

⚡ Power — one relay, the e-stop, and a diode that earns its keep

The whole kart hangs off one relay. Its coil is fed through the main switch and then through the emergency-stop mushroom (NC); the contact carries everything — the 5 V buck that feeds the ESP32 and the LED strip, and the motor driver's VB+. Open either one and the coil drops: motors, brain, lights, all of it, in the relay's own 10–20 ms.

The relay is a polarity gate — that is the reverse-polarity protection. Nothing on this kart is powered until the contact closes, and the contact only closes if the coil is energized. A relay coil does not care which way current flows through it, so a 1N4007 in series in the coil loop decides for it: with the battery backwards the diode blocks, the coil sees nothing, the contact stays open, and no current reaches anything — not the motor driver (no reverse protection of its own, destroyed instantly by reversed VB+), not the buck, not the ESP32. Nothing blows and nothing is stressed; put the battery back the right way and the kart starts. Fit it band toward the relay — backwards, the relay simply never closes, which is a loud and harmless way to fail.

The second diode, across the coil, is the flyback. When either switch opens the coil, the collapsing field would otherwise break a few hundred volts across the contact that just opened, eroding the mushroom and the main switch. Cathode to +, it does nothing in normal operation. It could almost serve as the polarity guard on its own — reversed, it shorts the coil and the relay does stay open — but it would do it by blowing the 40 A fuse and taking a surge far past its 1 A rating, and a diode that fails open takes the protection with it, silently. Hence the series one: same protection, passive, repeatable, and it costs ~0.9 V on a coil that pulls in around 7–8 V.

The firmware has no say in any of this. There is no power latch and no coil sense: the ESP32 cannot hold its own supply and cannot cut it. Press the mushroom and the brain dies with the motors — which is why nothing on this page ever reports "emergency stop engaged" on this page any more, and no self power-off after an idle period. The kart is switched off the way a lawnmower is: by hand.

No buttons either. The arming button on GPIO16 is gone with the rest of the GPIO inputs: arming is a ~1 s hold on the gamepad's START/Options button, stick centred — which is what the driver had in hand anyway, and which nobody standing beside the kart can press. The ESP32 now uses no input pin at all: four motor pins and one LED data line, all outputs.

Always 12 V, never measured. One 12 V motorcycle battery, which is also the motors' nominal voltage — so there is nothing to scale down and nothing to cap. The ADS1115 converter, the 100 k/15 k divider, the low-voltage cutoff and the 12/24 V detection are all gone; the remaining power ceiling is the single duty_cap setting. Watch the charge on the battery's own indicator, off the kart.
