💡 LED strip — what the colors mean

Moving rainbow = armed & ready (driving allowed) · yellow = healthy but disarmed (pulses while the START hold is in progress) · blinking red = fault stop (2 s rapid burst, then 1 Hz until cleared — never solid) · blue = gamepad calibration in progress.

🎓 Control modes

The kart turns the stick into a left/right motor command (arcade mixing: forward drives both wheels, turn adds a left/right difference). What sits between the stick and the motors is what changes between the two modes.

🔒 Closed loop — normal driving

Every safety and comfort layer is active; the encoders measure wheel speed.

🔧 Open loop — bench / stand testing

Raw passthrough: only the PWM cap and the safety gates remain — no speed limit, no rollover protection, no smoothing, no PID braking.

⚙️ Gear & sprocket ratios — motor to wheel, per side

Each rear (driven) wheel has its own drivetrain: a printed two-stage gearbox, then a #35 roller chain to the wheel. What makes two reductions fit in one small box is the compound gear — the 80T of stage 1 and the 30T of stage 2 are a single printed part on one shaft. Plan view; gears and sprockets are drawn proportional to their pitch diameters at the real centre distances, only the chain run is shortened to fit:

Two things the drawing is telling you. The two 80T overlap because they really do overlap seen from above — they live in different tooth planes, one above the other, which is what sets the depth of the housing and why nothing may stick out into the neighbouring plane. And the 25T sprocket is nearly as big as the 80T gear next to it: #35 chain has a 3/8″ (9.53 mm) pitch against the gearbox's 1.06 mm module, so a sprocket tooth is roughly nine times the pitch of a gear tooth.

Speed: 4615 rpm ÷ 17.07 ≈ 270 rpm at the wheel, × π × 0.254 m ≈ 3.6 m/s unloaded at 12 V (~3.4 m/s in practice) — well above what the firmware allows, the real cap is speed_limit_ms. Torque multiplies the same way in reverse: ~0.36 N·m at the motor becomes ~6 N·m at the wheel, which is why stage 2 is the thick one.

Why a chain and not a belt. A chain is cut to whatever length you need — add or drop links, close it with a master link — so the gearbox-to-wheel centre distance stays a free variable instead of being dictated by the belt lengths that happen to exist. On a one-off build where the motor mount finds its final position during assembly, that settles it. The counterpart: unlike a belt a chain does not damp shocks (they go straight into the gear teeth) and it wants lubrication, alignment and re-tensioning — a slack chain climbs the sprocket flank and is thrown.

⚡ Power — one relay, the e-stop, and a diode that earns its keep

The whole kart hangs off one relay. Its coil is fed through the main switch and then through the emergency-stop mushroom (NC); the contact carries everything — the 5 V buck that feeds the ESP32 and the LED strip, and the motor driver's VB+. Open either one and the coil drops: motors, brain, lights, all of it, in the relay's own 10–20 ms.

The diode is there for the coil, and it protects the whole kart by accident. Its job is flyback: when the coil is de-energized it would otherwise kick a few hundred volts across whatever just opened, and that arc eats switch contacts. Cathode to +, it does nothing at all in normal operation. Connect the battery backwards, though, and that same diode is suddenly forward-biased across the coil: it shorts it, so the relay never pulls in — and because everything hangs off that one contact, no current reaches the system at all. Not the motor driver (no reverse protection of its own, destroyed instantly by reversed VB+), not the buck, not the ESP32, not the sensors. Afterwards: fix the polarity, replace the diode and check the 40 A fuse — the two race each other during the event, and a relay that no longer clicks is a dead diode, not a dead relay.

The firmware has no say in any of this. There is no power latch and no coil sense: the ESP32 cannot hold its own supply and cannot cut it. Press the mushroom and the brain dies with the motors — which is why there is no "emergency stop engaged" fault on this page any more, and no self power-off after an idle period. The kart is switched off the way a lawnmower is: by hand.

No buttons either. The arming button on GPIO16 is gone with the rest of the GPIO inputs: arming is a ~1 s hold on the gamepad's START/Options button, stick centred — which is what the driver had in hand anyway, and which nobody standing beside the kart can press. The ESP32 now uses no input pin at all: two I²C buses in, four motor pins and one LED data line out.

Always 12 V, never measured. One 12 V motorcycle battery, which is also the motors' nominal voltage — so there is nothing to scale down and nothing to cap. The ADS1115 converter, the 100 k/15 k divider, the low-voltage cutoff and the 12/24 V detection are all gone; the remaining power ceiling is the single duty_cap setting. Watch the charge on the battery's own indicator, off the kart.
